Rain washes out India vs New Zealand 3rd ODI in Hamilton — India keep 2-0 series lead

No ball was bowled at Seddon Park as persistent rain abandoned the third ODI; India cannot lose the five-match series, and New Zealand must win both remaining games in Mount Maunganui to level it.

By Sheryar Khan • 11 Nov 2026

The third ODI between New Zealand and India at Seddon Park in Hamilton was abandoned without a ball being bowled on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, after persistent rain and a wet outfield made play impossible. The washout leaves India 2-0 ahead in the five-match series with two games to play — and, crucially, it means India can no longer lose the series. New Zealand can still level it 2-2, but only by winning both remaining matches at Bay Oval in Mount Maunganui.

The day never really got going. The toss was originally scheduled for 2:30 PM local time but was delayed by more than two hours because of rain and a wet outfield. Officials reduced the match to 40 overs a side, and when the captains finally walked out, India captain Shubman Gill won the toss for the third successive time in the series and chose to bowl. The covers came off around 4 PM local time and there was brief hope of a start, but the rain returned, and after further ground inspections the umpires called the match off at about 6:41 PM local time — around 70 minutes before the 7:50 PM cut-off for a 20-over-a-side game. The forecast had given a 90 per cent chance of rain through the day, and a sell-out crowd at Seddon Park went home without seeing a single delivery.

India made a strong start to the ODI series, and rain has shaped every match so far. In the opening ODI on 4 November at Eden Park in Auckland, New Zealand were bowled out for 185 in 40.2 overs — Arshdeep Singh took four wickets, Gurnoor Brar 3 for 44 and Anshul Kamboj 2 for 22 — and India chased a revised target of 143 in 32 overs, reaching 143 for 5 in 22.4 overs to win by five wickets on the Duckworth-Lewis-Stern (DLS) method. Virat Kohli made 74 off 51 balls in that win. The second ODI on 7 November in Wellington was another low-scoring, rain-hit affair: New Zealand were bowled out for 134 in 41 overs, Daryl Mitchell making 55 and Brar taking 4 for 30, and India were 113 for 7 in 26 overs when rain stopped play for good — four runs ahead of the DLS par score of 109 — to win by four runs on DLS.

The tour began with five T20 Internationals from 22 October to 1 November, and India won that series 3-2. India won the first three matches — by four wickets, four wickets and six wickets — before New Zealand hit back with wins in the last two games, by 87 runs and seven wickets. The ODI series that followed has been just as rain-affected, and the weather has now decided two of the three results in its own way.

The teams now travel to Mount Maunganui for the final two ODIs, both at Bay Oval — the fourth on Friday, 13 November, and the fifth on Sunday, 15 November. The fourth ODI is the big one: another Indian victory would seal the series, while New Zealand must win both games to square it 2-2. A series win in New Zealand would mean a lot for India — they have waited seven years for an ODI series victory on New Zealand soil, and this washout has kept that chance alive without them having to win another game.

After the ODIs, the tour moves into two Tests: the first from 19 to 23 November at Basin Reserve in Wellington, and the second from 27 November to 1 December at Hagley Oval in Christchurch. For now, though, all eyes are on Bay Oval and the weather. India will enter the fourth ODI full of confidence after taking a 2-0 lead, while New Zealand will hope to use home conditions to their advantage — they need strong batting partnerships and disciplined bowling to challenge India's position. Weather could again play a part after rain affected all three ODIs so far, and fans will be hoping for better conditions in Mount Maunganui so both teams can complete the remaining matches.

For New Zealand, the arithmetic is now simple and unforgiving. They must win in Mount Maunganui on Friday and again on Sunday — anything less and the series is India's. Captain Mitchell Santner, who has now lost the toss in all three ODIs, will want his batters to put up a total his bowlers can defend, because the first two games showed how quickly rain can take a chase out of New Zealand's hands. India, meanwhile, know that one more win gives them the series, and a 3-0 lead with a game to spare would be their first ODI series win in New Zealand since 2019.
