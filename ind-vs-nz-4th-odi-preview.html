India vs New Zealand 4th ODI preview: India can seal the series at Bay Oval as Shreyas Iyer takes charge

India lead the five-match series 2-0 and a win at Bay Oval on Friday would wrap it up with a game to spare. New Zealand can no longer win the series — but they can still draw it. Shreyas Iyer captains India for the first time, with Shubman Gill, KL Rahul and Anshul Kamboj released for Test preparations.

By Sheryar Khan • 11 Nov 2026

India arrive at Bay Oval on Friday with the series in their hands. They lead the five-match ODI series against New Zealand 2-0, and a win in the fourth ODI would make it 3-0 and seal the series with a game to spare. New Zealand, after the rain ruined the third ODI in Hamilton, can no longer win the series — the best they can do now is draw it 2-2. The match is a day-night game at Bay Oval in Mount Maunganui, starting at 7:00 AM PKT (7:30 AM IST, 1:00 PM AEDT), and it comes with a new captain for India and a new-look batting order.

The series so far has been short on runs and long on rain. In the first ODI in Auckland on 4 November, New Zealand were bowled out for 185, and India chased down a revised target of 143 to win by five wickets under the DLS method, with Virat Kohli making 74 off 51 balls. Arshdeep Singh took four wickets and Gurnoor Brar took three for 44. In the second ODI in Wellington on 7 November, New Zealand managed only 134 — Daryl Mitchell top-scoring with 55, Brar taking four for 30 — and India won by four runs under DLS, reaching 113 for 7. The third ODI in Hamilton on Tuesday was abandoned without a ball bowled: Shubman Gill won the toss for the third successive time and elected to field, the game was reduced to 40 overs a side, and it was called off at around 6:41 PM local time because of rain and a wet outfield.

That washout at least ended New Zealand's run of 11 consecutive home bilateral ODI series victories — a record that had stood for years — because they can no longer win this series.

The big story of the fourth ODI is the change of captain. Gill, KL Rahul and fast bowler Anshul Kamboj have been released from the ODI squad to begin preparations for the two-Test series that follows, which means Shreyas Iyer will lead the side for the first time in the fourth ODI. It is a big moment for Iyer, who has been one of India's most reliable middle-order batters in white-ball cricket, and it comes with the series on the line.

India's likely XI is: Rohit Sharma, Ruturaj Gaikwad, Virat Kohli, Shreyas Iyer (captain), Ishan Kishan (wicketkeeper), Axar Patel, Naman Dhir, Auqib Nabi, Gurnoor Brar, Mohammed Siraj and Arshdeep Singh. There are two big holes in the batting with Gill and Rahul both unavailable — Gaikwad comes in to open, and the middle order will have to carry more of the load. Kuldeep Yadav is expected to remain on the bench, according to reports, and Tilak Varma will have to wait for his chance.

Bay Oval should be a relief after Hamilton. It is one of New Zealand's truest batting decks, with consistent bounce and even carry through the whole game, and a first-innings par score of around 280 to 295. Teams batting second have won 53% of the games here, thanks to the true bounce under lights. The weather is expected to be crisp and sunny, around 21 degrees, with light coastal breezes and no threat of rain — a welcome change after the washout.

India and New Zealand have played three ODIs at this ground, with India winning two and New Zealand one, and the highest successful chase here is 300 for 5 by New Zealand against India. In other words, this is a ground where runs come, and where a big chase is never out of the question.

The overall head-to-head record favours India. In 126 ODIs between the two sides, India have won 65, New Zealand 52, with one tie and eight no-results — the third ODI in Hamilton being the latest of those. Recent form tells the same story: India have won three of their last five completed ODIs, with one loss and one draw, while New Zealand have won one, lost three and drawn one.

New Zealand's batting has been the problem in this series — 185 and 134 in the two completed games — and they will need their top order to stand up at Bay Oval. Daryl Mitchell, who made 55 in Wellington, is the one New Zealand batter who has looked comfortable against India's attack. For India, the bowling has been the story: Brar has taken three for 44 and four for 30 in the two completed games, and Arshdeep took four wickets in Auckland. Kohli's 74 in the first ODI was the only fifty of the series so far, and he will be the key wicket for New Zealand again.

There is a bigger picture to this series, too. The T20I series that came before it was won 3-2 by India, from 22 October to 1 November, and after the ODIs the two teams play a two-Test series: the first Test from 19 to 23 November at Basin Reserve in Wellington, and the second from 27 November to 1 December at Hagley Oval in Christchurch. That is why Gill, Rahul and Kamboj have already left the ODI camp — the Tests matter more to both teams in the long run.

But the ODI series still matters, especially to New Zealand, who do not want to lose a home series 3-0 or 4-0 after the washout took away their chance of winning it. A win on Friday would keep the series alive and give them something to play for in the fifth ODI on Sunday, 15 November, at the same ground. A loss would end it, and the fifth ODI would become a dead rubber.

India start as favourites, and the numbers say they should be. They have the better form, the better bowling, and a captain in Iyer who will want to make his first game in charge a winning one. But New Zealand at home, with their backs to the wall and nothing to lose, are a different proposition — and Bay Oval, with its true bounce and short boundaries, is a ground where one good innings can change everything. The toss will matter, the first ten overs will matter, and after Hamilton, both teams will just be glad to get a game in. We will update this post with the result after the match.
