India vs New Zealand 2nd Test preview: Christchurch decider after Wellington draw

The two-Test series is level at 0-0 after the Wellington draw, and the second Test at Hagley Oval, Christchurch, from Friday 27 November, will decide it. New Zealand have lost Blair Tickner to injury; India look set to keep most of the side that made 720 for 9 declared.

By Sheryar Khan • 24 Nov 2026

India and New Zealand meet in the second and final Test at Hagley Oval, Christchurch, from Friday 27 November to Tuesday 1 December, with the two-match series level at 0-0 after the first Test in Wellington ended in a draw. Play starts at 11:00 am local time (3:00 am PKT), with the toss 30 minutes earlier. The winner takes the series; a draw would leave it shared 0-0, and both sides need the win for their World Test Championship hopes.

The first Test was a story of two huge batting efforts and a final-day block. New Zealand made 368 after being 70 for 5, with Henry Nicholls unbeaten on 175 and Glenn Phillips making 118 in a 216-run stand. India replied with 720 for 9 declared — six players passed fifty, with KL Rahul making 136 and Rishabh Pant 128. New Zealand then batted out 120 overs on the final day to finish 239 for 5, with Daryl Mitchell unbeaten on 49 from 186 balls and Phillips unbeaten on 26 from 126, and the captains shook hands with about an hour left.

New Zealand's preparations have been hit by injury. Fast bowler Blair Tickner has been ruled out of the second Test with a right hamstring injury picked up in training in Wellington, and Matt Fisher has been called into the 14-man squad. Tickner did not play in Wellington, but New Zealand are already short of bowlers: Mitchell Santner fractured his right hand batting in the fifth ODI at Mount Maunganui on 15 November and misses the whole series. Fisher, 27, has played one Test, against Zimbabwe in August 2025, and took six wickets in the ODI series against India at 13.66, with best figures of 3 for 31. New Zealand may change a pace attack that conceded 720 in Wellington, where Jacob Duffy finished with 1 for 201 from 33 overs.

India look set to stay largely unchanged. Captain Shubman Gill said after the draw: "Maybe we'll see how the wicket is, but I think most of the team is going to be the same." That leaves Mohammed Siraj, who sat out the first Test while Jasprit Bumrah, Akash Deep, Anshul Kamboj and Prasidh Krishna shared the seam bowling, waiting again. Gill called leaving Siraj out "a very tough decision" and said the call was made on current form.

Hagley Oval is a very different challenge from the Basin Reserve. The Christchurch pitch is known for seam movement and bounce, especially on the first morning, and captains who win the toss there have chosen to bowl in 14 of the last 15 Tests. India's only Test at the ground ended in a seven-wicket defeat in February-March 2020, when they were bowled out for 242 and 124.

The World Test Championship stakes are clear. The draw gave each side four points: New Zealand slipped to third in the table, with Australia moving up to second, while India remain fourth with a points percentage of 50. Gill said India can still reach the final: "If we play well in five out of the next six, I think we should be in a good position to qualify for the WTC Final."

Players to watch: Nicholls, the player of the match in Wellington with 175 not out and 31; Phillips, who made 118 and then 26 not out from 126 balls to save the match; Rahul and Pant, both centurions in India's 720; and Bumrah, who took 4 for 92 in New Zealand's first innings. The series is shown on Sony Sports Network and SonyLIV.
