Carrick's future decided today? Monaco meeting, Pochettino favourite — but keeping him is also on the table

REPORTS — Manchester United are set to sack Michael Carrick, The Athletic reports, with Mauricio Pochettino the favourite to replace him. The club insists no final decision has been reached.

Cricgoals — News Desk • 10 Nov 2026

REPORTS: This story is based on media reports. Manchester United have not confirmed any decision, and the club insists no final decision has been reached.

Manchester United's senior executives meet co-owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe in Monaco today (Wednesday, 11 Nov) to decide manager Michael Carrick's future, The Athletic reports. Chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox are expected to recommend a change, with Mauricio Pochettino reported as the favourite to take over. But reports also say United could keep Carrick and add coaches to support him — Bayern Munich's Aaron Danks is mentioned. Carrick took training on Tuesday and remains in charge, and a decision is expected today or Thursday, 12 Nov.

United are 18th in the Premier League after one win in 10 games, a position that would have been unthinkable for this club in any other era. The reports say the men running the football side of the club have concluded that waiting any longer helps no one: not the players, not the manager, and not a support that has watched one of the biggest clubs in world football drift into the bottom three.

A season that never got going

One win in 10 games tells its own story, but the manner of the results has hurt almost as much as the numbers. Sunday's 4-4 comeback draw against Aston Villa summed up the Carrick reign in ninety minutes — spirit and goals going forward, chaos at the back, and points dropped from positions a United side is expected to control. A comeback draw that feels like a defeat is a difficult thing to sell to a board, and a harder one to sell to fans who have seen the team slide to 18th place.

Carrick's position was not helped by the fact that the problems have repeated week after week. Defending set pieces, protecting leads, and starting matches with intensity have all been raised again and again, and each new match has brought the same questions rather than new answers. When a manager stops being able to change the story of a season, the story usually ends the same way — and, according to these reports, United's decision-makers have reached exactly that conclusion.

The Monaco meeting

The detail that gives this story its weight is where the decision is being made. Carrick took training at Carrington on Tuesday and remains in charge, while the club's chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox were preparing to meet co-owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe in Monaco today (Wednesday, 11 Nov) to decide his future. In modern football, sackings are rarely decided by one voice; they are built, layer by layer, by the executives who run the club day to day and approved by the ownership. On the reported timeline, that process is now at its final step.

The Liverpool match on 22 November hangs over everything. A new manager in place before that fixture would walk straight into one of the biggest matches in English football for a debut — a baptism that could define a reign before it has properly begun, for better or for worse. Waiting until after it, the reports suggest, was judged the worse option: better a fresh start before the biggest game than drift into it with a manager everyone believes is leaving.

The candidates: Pochettino favourite

Mauricio Pochettino is the favourite to replace Carrick, according to the reports. The United States head coach has a reported release clause in his USA contract, and his name has followed this job for years: a coach who builds teams around energy, youth and a clear identity, and who took Tottenham Hotspur to a Champions League final. If United want a proven Premier League builder rather than a short-term firefighter, Pochettino is the obvious name at the top of the list.

He is not the only name. Eddie Howe, Julian Nagelsmann and Antonio Conte are also linked with the job in the reports — three very different coaches, and three very different ideas of what United should become. Howe offers Premier League knowledge and player development; Nagelsmann offers the modern, high-intensity German school; Conte offers a proven title winner whose methods demand total backing. That the reported list is so varied tells you the club is weighing identity as much as availability.

A 148-year first

If the reports are right, this would be the first time in United's 148-year history that two permanent managers are sacked in the same calendar year. For a club that once defined stability — a club whose greatest era was built by giving one man time — that single statistic explains how far the present has drifted from the past. Managerial churn has become the rhythm of United's recent years, and each change has been sold as the fresh start; the uncomfortable question hanging over this one is why this time should be different.

What the club says — and what happens next

For now, officially, nothing has happened. The club insists no final decision has been reached, and everything above remains reporting — from The Athletic and other outlets — rather than confirmation. Carrick remains the manager, Carrington remains his workplace, and United's next official word will be the one that matters. But when a club's chief executive and director of football are reported to be recommending a sacking to the co-owner, the direction of travel is rarely reversed.

United supporters have been here before, and most will judge the decision not by the sacking itself but by what follows: the appointment, the plan, and whether the football finally starts to match the size of the club. Until the club confirms anything, this page will keep this story labelled as it should be — reports, not fact.

Question for readers: If Carrick goes, who should United appoint — Pochettino, Howe, Nagelsmann or Conte?
