New Zealand vs India 5th ODI: Arshdeep stars as India defend 209 to seal series 3-1

India defended a modest 209 at Bay Oval to beat New Zealand by 22 runs and clinch the ODI series 3-1 — their first series win in New Zealand in seven years.

By Sheryar Khan • 15 Nov 2026

MOUNT MAUNGANUI: India defended a modest total of 209 to beat New Zealand by 22 runs in the fifth and final ODI at Bay Oval on Sunday, sealing the five-match series 3-1 and their first ODI series win in New Zealand in seven years.

Top order fails again

After New Zealand captain Mitchell Santner won the toss and chose to bowl, India's top order collapsed for the second match in a row. Rohit Sharma fell for seven and Virat Kohli for five, and with Ruturaj Gaikwad gone for 18, India were 34 for 3. Stand-in captain Shreyas Iyer made 22, but the visitors kept losing wickets at regular intervals.

Kishan and Axar rebuild

Ishan Kishan and Axar Patel then steadied the innings, both scoring 46 — Kishan from 58 balls with three fours and two sixes, Axar from 55 balls with five fours and a six. Naman Dhir added 22, and a late flourish from the tail lifted India to 209: Arshdeep Singh smashed 14 from just four balls, including two sixes, and Mohammed Siraj finished unbeaten on 14 as the pair added 26 for the last wicket in 10 balls. Santner and Jacob Duffy took three wickets each, with Matthew Fisher claiming two.

Arshdeep leads the defence

Chasing 210, New Zealand never found a partnership. Siraj removed Tim Seifert for 14 and Finn Allen for one, while Arshdeep dismissed Will Young for 22 — Ruturaj Gaikwad taking a spectacular diving catch in the deep — and Nick Kelly for 24. Dean Foxcroft top-scored with 31 and Santner made 24, but India struck every time New Zealand threatened to rebuild. Debutant Yash Thakur dismissed Foxcroft, Naman Dhir removed Santner, and Axar Patel took the final wicket of Blair Tickner as New Zealand were bowled out for 187 in 48.1 overs. Arshdeep finished with 3 for 30 in 10 overs, including two maidens, and was named Player of the Match.

Series sealed — and the 244-run defeat forgotten

The win meant Friday's 244-run defeat in the fourth ODI — Will Young's 125 and Matt Henry's 6 for 27 — will go down as an aberration in a 3-1 series victory. It also ended New Zealand's 11-series unbeaten run in home bilateral ODI series, and gave India their first overseas ODI series win under head coach Gautam Gambhir. India had won the first two ODIs in Auckland and Wellington; the third, in Hamilton, was washed out by rain. The teams now meet in a two-match Test series, starting at Basin Reserve, Wellington, on Thursday 19 November.
