Pakistan declare 434-9, Sri Lanka 114-3 at Day 2 stumps — Saud 93, debutant Minhas 52, Saqib strikes twice

Saud Shakeel's 93, five fifties and a dream debut spell from Saqib Khan put Pakistan in total control in Rawalpindi — Sri Lanka closed Day 2 on 114-3, trailing by 320.

Cricgoals — News Desk • 10 Nov 2026

RAWALPINDI: Pakistan declared their first innings on 434-9 on Day 2 in Rawalpindi, after Saud Shakeel's 93 and debutant Arafat Minhas's 52, and then struck three times with the ball to leave Sri Lanka 114-3 at stumps, trailing by 320 runs. It was a day that belonged to Pakistan's present and future at the same time: an established middle-order batter fell seven short of a hundred, three debutants made fifties across the innings, and another debutant, Saqib Khan, took two wickets — including his maiden Test wicket — in his first day of bowling in Test cricket.

Pakistan had closed Day 1 on 281-5 after Sri Lanka won the toss and chose to bowl, and the second day was about turning a strong start into a commanding total. Five batters passed fifty in the innings — Babar Azam (50), debutants Tayyab Arif (57) and Mohammad Taha (51), Saud Shakeel (93) and Arafat Minhas (52) — a spread of contributions that kept the scoreboard moving in every session and never allowed Sri Lanka's bowlers to settle for long.

Saud falls seven short of a hundred

Saud Shakeel resumed on 57 not out and continued in the same calm, unhurried manner that has become his signature in Test cricket. He reached lunch on 91 not out from 192 balls, with Pakistan 390-6, but fell after the break for 93 — seven runs short of a century that had looked his for the taking all morning. It was still the innings that held Pakistan's total together: long, patient and timed so that the declaration could come with the game exactly where Pakistan wanted it.

Before Saud's dismissal, Ghazi Ghori had fallen for 48 — two short of his own fifty — to Dilshan Madushanka, with the score on 349-6. That wicket brought debutant Arafat Minhas to the crease, and the youngster played the kind of innings that selection dreams are made of: positive without being reckless, he reached 52 and made sure Pakistan's lower order kept adding runs deep into the day. By the time the declaration came at 434-9, Sri Lanka were facing not just a big total, but the pressure of batting last in the match with more than three days still to play.

Score summary: Pakistan 434-9 declared (Saud Shakeel 93, Tayyab Arif 57, Arafat Minhas 52, Mohammad Taha 51, Babar Azam 50, Ghazi Ghori 48) — Sri Lanka 114-3 (Lahiru Udara 42, Kamindu Mendis 40 not out; Saqib Khan 2-54). Sri Lanka trail by 320 runs.

A debut to remember for Saqib Khan

If the day had already given Pakistan plenty to smile about, Saqib Khan added the story that will be told in his family for years. Bowling in his first Test, the debutant took 2-54, and his maiden Test wicket was a name any bowler would be proud to have first on his record: Dinesh Chandimal, out for 1. Three Sri Lanka wickets fell before the close, and each one tightened Pakistan's grip on a match that has moved steadily in one direction since the first morning.

Sri Lanka's reply was carried, almost single-handedly, by two batters. Lahiru Udara made 42 before falling, and Kamindu Mendis was still there at the close on 40 not out — the one wicket Pakistan will want most when play resumes, because Mendis has built a reputation for making bowlers pay when he is left unbeaten overnight. Around them, though, there was little resistance, and Sri Lanka will resume on Wednesday still 320 runs behind with seven wickets in hand.

Five fifties: the platform that decided the day

What made Pakistan's total so hard for Sri Lanka to answer was not one great innings but the weight of numbers behind it. Arif's 57 and Taha's 51 on debut, Babar's 50 on the first day, and then Saud's 93 and Minhas's 52 on the second — every time Sri Lanka took a wicket, another Pakistan batter was already set or settled quickly. In a World Test Championship match, that kind of depth matters twice over: it wins sessions, and it gives the captain the freedom to declare early enough to attack with the ball while the pitch still has something in it.

What Day 3 holds

Day 3 resumes on Wednesday 11 November at 9:30 AM PKT, and Pakistan's plan will be simple to say and hard to stop: take the remaining seven wickets as cheaply as possible, keep the lead large, and leave Sri Lanka's batters with the scoreboard pressure that comes from a 320-run deficit. For Sri Lanka, everything rests on Mendis and the men below him — bat through the day, and the match lives; lose early wickets, and Rawalpindi could see a result long before the fifth day.

This is the first of two Tests in the series, with the second Test in Rawalpindi from 17-21 November. Both matches count for the World Test Championship 2025-27, which means every session in Rawalpindi carries points as well as pride — and on the evidence of the first two days, it is Pakistan who are collecting both.
