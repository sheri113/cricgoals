Day 3 stumps in Rawalpindi — Sri Lanka 404-4, Dhananjaya 167 not out, Kusal 117 not out, trail by 30

Dhananjaya de Silva and Kusal Mendis batted through the rest of Day 3 with unbeaten centuries and an unbeaten 249-run stand, taking Sri Lanka to 404 for 4 at stumps — just 30 runs behind Pakistan's 434 for 9 declared. Bad light ended play early, with 23 overs still to be bowled.

By Sheryar Khan • 11 Nov 2026

Sri Lanka reached 120 for 3 in the first session of Day 3 of the first Test in Rawalpindi on Wednesday, 11 November, still trailing Pakistan's 434 for 9 declared by 314 runs. Kamindu Mendis is 40 not out from 88 balls and captain Dhananjaya de Silva is 28 not out from 39, the pair having added 55 runs for the fourth wicket. Sri Lanka resumed the day on 114 for 3, and the morning has been a watchful one — just six runs in the first three overs of the day, with Pakistan's bowlers keeping things tight. Mohammad Abbas has figures of 10 overs, five maidens, 23 runs and one wicket, and debutant Saqib Khan has two for 53. The key number for the visitors is 235: that is the total they must reach to avoid the follow-on, and they are still 115 runs short of it with seven wickets in hand.

After lunch, the two Sri Lankan batters simply did not give Pakistan a way back in. Dhananjaya de Silva, who was 87 not out at lunch, went on to an unbeaten 167 off 241 balls with 20 fours — his 14th Test hundred. Kusal Mendis, captaining Sri Lanka in a Test for the first time, reached his hundred off 152 balls with a ramped boundary over the wicketkeeper's head and finished the day on 117 not out off 188 balls, with 10 fours and four sixes. Their unbeaten fifth-wicket stand is now worth 249 runs off 360 balls, and Sri Lanka lost only one wicket in the whole day — Kamindu Mendis, caught by Ghazi Ghori off Mohammad Abbas for 58, when the score was 155 for 4. Pakistan's bowlers toiled without reward after lunch: Abbas finished the day with 2 for 64 and debutant Saqib Khan with 2 for 105. Play ended at 4:15 in the afternoon, when the light dropped quickly and the umpires took a reading before taking the players off — 23 overs of the day were still to be bowled. Sri Lanka go into Day 4 trailing by just 30 runs with six wickets in hand, and the follow-on is no longer a question. On Thursday morning (Day 4 starts at 9:30 AM PKT), Sri Lanka will look to take the lead and build a first-innings advantage, while Pakistan will need early wickets to keep the deficit small and stay in the match.

Day 2 had belonged almost entirely to Pakistan. After closing Day 1 on 281 for 5, the hosts declared their first innings on 434 for 9, built on Saud Shakeel's 93 and debutant Arafat Minhas's 52. Five batters hit fifties in the innings — Babar Azam (50), debutants Tayyab Arif (57) and Mohammad Taha (51), Saud (93) and Minhas (52) — and Ghazi Ghori added 48. For Sri Lanka, Asitha Fernando took 3 for 107 and Dilshan Madushanka 3 for 108. Pakistan then struck three times with the ball before stumps: debutant Saqib Khan took 2 for 54, including the maiden Test wicket of Dinesh Chandimal for 1, and Mohammad Abbas took 1 for 23. Lahiru Udara made 42 before falling to Saqib, caught by Ghazi Ghori, and at stumps Sri Lanka were 114 for 3, trailing by 320. Nearly 45 overs were lost to bad light across the day, and play was called off around the same time as the light faded.

The morning session on Day 3 has been about survival for Sri Lanka. Kamindu Mendis, unbeaten on 40 overnight, has batted with the patience the situation demands, and Dhananjaya de Silva has grown into his innings after resuming on 22. Their partnership of 55 has now passed fifty, and it is the stand on which Sri Lanka's hopes of saving this Test rest. If this pair can bat through to lunch, Sri Lanka will be well on the way to the follow-on target; if Pakistan break through early, the middle order will be exposed to a fresh ball and a home attack full of confidence. The pitch at Rawalpindi has offered something for the bowlers without being unplayable, and the first hour has shown that runs will have to be earned.

Pakistan will be the happier side, and they know it. A first-innings lead of 434 was built patiently, session by session, and the bowlers have backed it up. Saqib Khan's debut has been one of the stories of the match — two wickets on Day 2, including a maiden Test scalp — and Mohammad Abbas has been his usual metronomic self. The hosts will want at least one wicket before lunch to open up the middle order, and with the second new ball not far away, the pressure on the two set batters will only grow. For Sri Lanka, the task is clear and simple to state, hard to do: bat long, get to 235, and make Pakistan bat again.

There is also news about the series beyond this match. The second Test, scheduled for 17 to 21 November, has been moved from Lahore to Rawalpindi because of smog in Lahore — the PCB announced the change on Tuesday, 10 November — so both Tests of the series will now be played at the Rawalpindi Cricket Stadium. Both matches count for the World Test Championship 2025-27. For now, though, all attention is on this first Test and on whether Kamindu and Dhananjaya can keep Pakistan at bay through the morning.

One more thing to watch is the light. Bad light has already cost this match nearly 45 overs on Day 2, and the players left the field around the same time on both days when the light faded. If the light holds, Pakistan will want to press home their advantage before the close; if it fades again, Sri Lanka will be grateful for every over that is lost. The weather in Rawalpindi has been kind so far this morning, but the light, not the rain, has been the real enemy of this Test.
