Pakistan vs Sri Lanka 1st Test Day 4: Sri Lanka 404-4, just 30 behind — Dhananjaya 167*, Kusal 117*, rain threatens

Sri Lanka resume Day 4 on 404 for 4, just 30 runs behind Pakistan's 434 for 9 declared, with Dhananjaya de Silva and Kusal Mendis unbeaten on 167 and 117. Showers are expected from late morning, and heavy afternoon rain could decide the day.

By Sheryar Khan • 12 Nov 2026

Day 4 of the first Test starts in Rawalpindi on Thursday, 12 November, at 9:30 AM PKT, and Sri Lanka are in a strong position. The visitors resume on 404 for 4, just 30 runs behind Pakistan's 434 for 9 declared, with six wickets in hand and two set batters at the crease. Dhananjaya de Silva is unbeaten on 167 from 241 balls, and captain Kusal Mendis is unbeaten on 117 from 188, and their unbroken fifth-wicket stand is worth 249 runs. Sri Lanka need only 31 more runs to take the lead, and from there they can build a first-innings advantage that would put real pressure on Pakistan.

The partnership has been the story of this Test. Dhananjaya, who was 87 not out at lunch on Day 3, batted through the rest of the day without giving Pakistan a chance, hitting 20 fours in his unbeaten 167. Kusal, captaining Sri Lanka in a Test for the first time, reached his hundred with a ramped boundary over the wicketkeeper's head and finished the day on 117 not out, with 10 fours and four sixes. Sri Lanka lost only one wicket in the whole of Day 3 — Kamindu Mendis, caught by Ghazi Ghori off Mohammad Abbas for 58, when the score was 155 for 4. Pakistan's bowlers toiled without reward after lunch: Abbas finished the day with 2 for 64 and debutant Saqib Khan with 2 for 105.

For Pakistan, the morning session is everything. They need early wickets — ideally both set batters before lunch — to keep the deficit small and stay in the match. The second new ball is not far away, and with it comes the best chance to break through. Mohammad Abbas has been the most threatening bowler, and Saqib Khan's debut has already produced two wickets, including the maiden Test scalp of Dinesh Chandimal. But if Dhananjaya and Kusal bat through the first session, Sri Lanka will go past Pakistan's total and the hosts will be chasing the game.

The weather may have the final say. Showers are expected in Rawalpindi from around 11 AM, with light rain by midday and heavy rain in the afternoon. Bad light has already cost this match heavily — 23 overs were lost on Day 3 when play ended at 4:15 in the afternoon, and nearly 45 overs were lost to bad light on Day 2. If the rain arrives as forecast, the match looks headed for a draw, with Sri Lanka in the stronger position. The light, not the bowling, has been the real enemy of this Test.

Beyond this match, the series continues with the second Test from 17 to 21 November, also in Rawalpindi after it was moved from Lahore because of smog. Both matches count for the World Test Championship 2025-27. For now, though, all attention is on whether Sri Lanka can turn their strong position into a first-innings lead, and whether Pakistan can find a way through before the weather closes in.
