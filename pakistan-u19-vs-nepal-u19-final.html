Pakistan U19 beat Nepal U19 by 101 runs — Farhan 74 leads defending champions to big win

Defending champions Pakistan U19 finished their Group A campaign in style at Al Amerat, posting 275-7 and bowling Nepal U19 out for 174 to win by 101 runs.

By Sheryar Khan • 10 Nov 2026

AL AMERAT, OMAN: Defending champions Pakistan U19 beat Nepal U19 by 101 runs in their final Group A match of the ACC Men's Under-19 Asia Cup 2026 at Al Amerat, Oman — a complete performance with bat and ball that underlined why this team arrived at the tournament as the side everyone wants to beat. Pakistan posted 275-7, powered by a 135-run stand between captain Farhan Yousaf (74 off 95) and Ahmed Hussain, and then bowled Nepal out for 174 in 43.4 overs.

Nepal won the toss and chose to bowl, backing their bowlers to make early use of the conditions — and for the first part of the morning, the decision looked a good one. What followed, though, was the passage of play that decided the match: a captain's innings, a partnership worth 135, and a total that was always going to ask too much of Nepal's young batting line-up.

Early trouble at 64-3

Pakistan's innings did not begin like a 275-run innings. Usman Khan fell for 19 and Jahangir Bilal for 22, and when Abdul Qadir was dismissed for 6, Pakistan were 64-3 and Nepal were the happier dressing room. At under-19 level, a collapse from there is a familiar story: young batters, a big occasion, and a bowling side with its tails up. It is exactly the situation in which a captain earns his place — not with speeches, but with runs.

Farhan and Ahmed take the game away

Farhan Yousaf and Ahmed Hussain rebuilt first, and then they attacked. Their partnership of 135 runs changed the entire shape of the match: the field spread, Nepal's bowlers were rotated without reward, and the scoreboard moved from repair job to statement. Farhan's 74 came from 95 balls — an innings paced like a senior professional's, built on placement and patience rather than power, and all the more valuable because it began at 64-3 with the match in the balance.

By the time the stand was broken, Pakistan were on their way to 275-7, a total that gave their bowlers something every attack wants in a knockout-style group game: runs to defend, and the freedom to bowl attacking lines. In youth tournaments, where one bad hour can end a campaign, a near-276 target in a final group match is a mountain — and Nepal's chase never really got above base camp.

Nepal's chase never takes off

Chasing 276, Nepal needed a start; they never got one that lasted. Pakistan's bowlers kept the pressure on through the innings, wickets fell at the moments Nepal could least afford them, and the asking rate climbed until the chase became a formality. Nepal were bowled out for 174 in 43.4 overs — all out with more than six overs unused, which tells its own story about the gap between the sides on the day. The margin, 101 runs, is the kind of number that sends a message beyond one group table.

Score summary: Pakistan U19 275-7 (Farhan Yousaf 74 off 95; 135-run partnership with Ahmed Hussain) beat Nepal U19 174 all out in 43.4 overs by 101 runs. Nepal won the toss and chose to bowl. Al Amerat, Oman — ACC Men's Under-19 Asia Cup 2026, Group A.

What it means for the title defence

Pakistan came into this tournament as defending champions, and defending a title at under-19 level is harder than winning one: squads change completely, reputations count for nothing, and every opponent raises its game against the holders. Finishing the group stage with a 101-run win — built on a recovery from 64-3, a captain's seventy and a ruthless bowling performance — is the strongest possible way to close out Group A, a group that also contains India and the UAE.

There is a bigger picture, too. Under-19 Asia Cups are where Pakistan's next senior players first introduce themselves, and a captain who scores 74 off 95 in a pressure match, and a middle order that can build a 135-run stand after losing three early wickets, are exactly the signs coaches at every level look for. Nepal, for their part, will take lessons rather than points from Al Amerat — but bowling a side like Pakistan into early trouble at 64-3 is something their young attack can genuinely build on.

For now, the defending champions move on with confidence, a settled batting order and a captain in form. Group A is done; the hard part of the title defence starts next.

There is also the manner of the win to consider. Pakistan did not scrape home in a tight, low-scoring game; they recovered from early trouble, posted the kind of total that takes a match away from an opponent, and then closed it out with overs unused. In a group that also contains India and the UAE, a result like this does more than settle one afternoon — it sets the standard every other side in Group A now has to match, and it sends the defending champions forward with a settled batting order and a bowling attack full of confidence. Nepal, meanwhile, leave Al Amerat knowing they had the champions 64-3 at one stage; learning to turn starts like that into results is the next step in their own story.
