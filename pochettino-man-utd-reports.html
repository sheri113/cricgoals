Pochettino to Man Utd: Manchester United finalising deal to appoint him — Reports

Manchester United are finalising an agreement with Mauricio Pochettino to become their next head coach, according to reports, with Sir Jim Ratcliffe set to make the final call on Michael Carrick's future after a meeting in Monaco. The club has not confirmed anything.

By Sheryar Khan • 11 Nov 2026

REPORTS: This story is based on media reports. Manchester United have not confirmed any decision about Michael Carrick or Mauricio Pochettino.

Manchester United are finalising a deal to appoint Mauricio Pochettino as their next head coach, according to reports on Wednesday. The Times reports that United are in talks with Pochettino to finalise an agreement for him to take over, should Michael Carrick be sacked. The Athletic's David Ornstein reports that the club are "finalising a deal" for Pochettino to become their next head coach, with personal terms being ironed out with his representatives and "no issues expected". Sky Sports reports that United have reached a "verbal agreement in principle" with the Argentine. Everything, though, still depends on one thing: the "ultimate decision" to relieve Carrick of his duties. Nothing has been confirmed by the club.

That decision is being made in Monaco. Chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox flew out on Monday for a pre-planned meeting with co-owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe, who has a home in the principality. The two executives are expected to recommend that Carrick be dismissed and a new manager appointed. Ratcliffe will make the final call, but a final decision may not come until Thursday — around 24 hours before Pochettino's pre-Haiti press conference. One reason for the delay is that Ratcliffe would want to relay the information to Carrick face-to-face, which could push any official announcement to Thursday.

Carrick, meanwhile, is still waiting to be informed. He took first-team training at Carrington on Tuesday and again on Wednesday, with a reduced group because of international break commitments, and there has been no formal communication from the club about his future.

Pochettino's situation is complicated by his contract with the United States. He signed a new deal with US Soccer after the World Cup, only about three months ago, keeping him there until 2030. That contract contains a release clause reported at around £7.5 million by The Times, and around $10 million by other reports, with a further $2 million or so expected for his backroom staff. United have been in talks with his representatives to agree personal terms, but they are yet to speak to US Soccer about activating the clause.

Pochettino is said to want the job and is interested in leaving his role with the US team to return to the Premier League. He is currently preparing the United States for a two-legged Concacaf Nations League quarter-final against Haiti, on Saturday and Wednesday, and he is expected to address the media this week — when he is likely to be quizzed about the United links rather than the Haiti fixture.

Carrick's side of the story is a difficult one. According to one report, the manager "feels disrespected" that the club's co-owners have been leaking to the media that they will sack him, while he has yet to receive any direct explanation from the club. He was handed the job on a permanent basis in the summer of 2026, after a successful interim spell in which he led United to third place in the Premier League last season, and he signed a two-year contract in May. Since then the season has collapsed. United sit 18th in the table after their worst start to a season in 40 years, on an eight-match winless run, and the club's chiefs are said to have concluded that Carrick and his assistant, Steve Holland, seem incapable of arresting the slide and guiding the team away from the relegation zone.

Keeping Carrick and adding coaches to his staff — Bayern Munich's Aaron Danks has been mentioned — is still on the table as an option, according to reports, but the direction of travel now looks clear.

The cost of the change will be large. The appointment of Pochettino, plus the cost of sacking Carrick, who signed a two-year contract in May, will take the total the club have spent on hiring and firing managers since Sir Alex Ferguson left in 2013 to more than £100 million. That figure tells its own story about the last thirteen years at Old Trafford: no permanent manager since Ferguson has lasted, and the club are now on the verge of making another change in the middle of a season that is already drifting badly.

Why Pochettino? He is the clear favourite, and United have wanted him before. The 54-year-old built his reputation at Tottenham Hotspur, where he took the club to a Champions League final and made them regular title challengers, and he has since managed Paris Saint-Germain, Chelsea and the United States. He is known for developing young players and for a high-pressing, high-energy style of football — the kind of identity United have been searching for since Ferguson left. He is also available in the sense that his US contract has a release clause, which makes the move possible in the middle of a season, even if it is expensive and complicated.

What happens next is a matter of days, not weeks. A final decision is expected on Thursday, and United have the rest of the two-week international break to resolve the situation before returning to action at bitter rivals Liverpool on 22 November. If Pochettino is appointed, he will have very little time to prepare for that game — and he will arrive at a club in the relegation zone, with a squad low on confidence and a fanbase running out of patience. For Carrick, the wait continues: he is still the manager, still taking training, and still waiting for the club to tell him what it has decided.

For United, the Monaco meeting was supposed to end the uncertainty. The reports say the ending is now close — but until the club says so, it remains a report, not a fact.
