Rising Stars semi-finals: unbeaten Sri Lanka A face India A, Shaheens meet Bangladesh A

Sri Lanka A meet India A in the first semi-final on Thursday, 26 November, and defending champions Pakistan Shaheens face Bangladesh A on Friday, 27 November — both in Doha, both starting at 3:00 pm PKT.

By Sheryar Khan • 25 Nov 2026

DOHA: The group stage of the ACC Men's Asia Cup Rising Stars 2026 is over, and the semi-final line-up is set: Sri Lanka A face India A on Thursday, 26 November, and Pakistan Shaheens meet Bangladesh A on Friday, 27 November, both at the West End Park International Cricket Stadium in Doha, starting at 3:00 pm PKT.

Sri Lanka A go into the first semi-final unbeaten. They won all three Group A matches, finishing with a seven-wicket win over Bangladesh A on Monday — bowling Bangladesh out for 145 and chasing it down in just 21.5 overs, with Nishan Madushka unbeaten on 51 and captain Pavan Rathnayake making 45. Their spinners did the damage: Ravindu Fernando took 4 for 30, Wanuja Sahan 3 for 20 and Vijayakanth Viyaskanth 2 for 22.

India A stand in their way, and they carry the most destructive innings of the tournament. Fifteen-year-old Vaibhav Sooryavanshi smashed 204 off 53 balls against UAE on Tuesday — the fastest List A double-century ever, in 52 balls — as India A chased 250 in just 14.2 overs. But India A lost their only group match to Pakistan Shaheens, by 48 runs, and the question for Thursday is whether the rest of the batting and the bowling can match Sooryavanshi's firepower against an unbeaten side. Sooryavanshi has since revealed he spoke to his idol Yuvraj Singh for half an hour the night before that innings, after struggling in his first two matches — Yuvraj told him that if he played 20 balls, the score would be different.

The second semi-final is a repeat of last year's final. Defending champions Pakistan Shaheens, unbeaten in Group B with three wins from three, face Bangladesh A — the side they beat in a Super Over to win the title last time. The Shaheens beat UAE by 61 runs, India A by 48 runs and Nepal by 96 runs, finishing with a net run rate of +1.367.

Pakistan's win over Nepal showed both sides of this team. Bowled out for 168 after collapsing to 66 for 6, they were rescued by Rohail Nazir's unbeaten 64 — and then their bowlers dismissed Nepal for just 72. Sufyan Moqim took 3 for 17 and Mohammad Hasnain 3 for 21. Bangladesh A, meanwhile, won two of their three group matches, beating Hong Kong and Afghanistan A before losing to Sri Lanka A.

The winners meet in the final on Sunday, 29 November, at the same ground. And because the semi-finals cross the groups over, India A and Pakistan Shaheens can only meet again in that final — a rematch of their group match, which Pakistan won by 48 runs, is on the table if both win. Friday is a busy day for cricket fans: the second semi-final starts the same day as the India vs New Zealand second Test in Christchurch.
